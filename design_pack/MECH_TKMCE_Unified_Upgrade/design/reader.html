Heat conduction

Conduction is heat transfer through a material due to a temperature difference. Heat flows from a region of higher temperature toward a region of lower temperature.

Fourier’s law

For one-dimensional steady conduction through a plane wall with constant thermal conductivity, the magnitude of the heat transfer rate is:

Q̇ = kA(T₁ − T₂) / L

k — thermal conductivity (W/m·K)
A — cross-sectional area (m²)
L — wall thickness (m)

Worked example

A wall with k = 0.8 W/m·K, area 2 m², thickness 0.1 m and temperature difference 20 K transfers heat at 320 W under the stated assumptions.
